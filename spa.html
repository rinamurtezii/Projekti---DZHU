Spa or Mischief?

By PawCare Team • January 2026

During bath time, some pups turn into little water ninjas — splashing, spinning, and acting like the tub is a battlefield. But behind the chaos, there’s a reason: bath time can feel unfamiliar, slippery, and stressful for many dogs.

The good news? With a few simple steps, you can turn the “panic splashes” into a calmer, safer routine — and maybe even something your pup starts to enjoy.

Why bath time feels scary

Loud water, strong smells, and the sensation of being wet can overwhelm your dog’s senses. Some pups also had negative experiences in the past, so they associate baths with discomfort.

Make it easier: quick tips

  • Use lukewarm water and keep the spray gentle.
  • Place a non-slip mat or towel in the tub to prevent sliding.
  • Start slow: wet paws first, then gradually rinse the body.
  • Keep shampoo away from eyes and ears.
  • Reward calm moments with treats and praise.
“A calm bath starts before the water — with comfort, patience, and trust.”

When to skip the bath

If your dog has open wounds, severe skin irritation, or seems in pain, it’s better to pause and ask a vet or groomer first. Also, if your pup’s fear is intense, gentle desensitization over time can work better than forcing it in one day.

Tip: Keep bath sessions short. A quick rinse done calmly is better than a long bath that becomes stressful.
